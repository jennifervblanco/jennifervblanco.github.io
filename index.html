Behind the Click

How I think about a landing page.

Every element on a page is a decision that either helps a visitor act or gets in their way. Here is one page, taken apart. This is a teaching example, not a specific client result.

One clear promise, in plain words.
A single line on the outcome, not the feature list.
Trusted by teams like yours
Start the conversation
  1. 1

    One job per screen Clarity

    The headline states the offer and the outcome together, so a visitor decides in about three seconds whether to keep reading. A page that tries to say everything says nothing.

  2. 2

    Proof sits next to the ask Trust

    Doubt peaks right before someone commits, so the evidence, a result or a recognizable name, belongs beside the form, not three sections earlier where it is already forgotten.

  3. 3

    One primary action Conversion

    A single button, repeated, with nothing competing beside it. Every extra link is a chance to leave, so the page keeps the choice down to yes or not yet.

  4. 4

    The form asks for the minimum Friction

    Every field costs completed forms. Ask for what a first conversation genuinely needs, and leave the rest for later, once there is a reason to give it.
