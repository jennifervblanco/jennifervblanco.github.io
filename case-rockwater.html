When a respected firm is hard to find online

Rockwater had the reputation. The website did not show it. I rebuilt how the business gets discovered and turned traffic into a measurable source of conversations.

The challengeThe problem

Rockwater was winning on reputation and word of mouth, but almost nothing online reflected that strength. The site ranked for the company name and little else, paid spend ran without a clear story, and there was no reliable way to tell which marketing produced an actual conversation.

The brief was simple to say and harder to do: make the website earn its keep.

My roleWhat I owned

I led the digital marketing work end to end, from the search and content strategy to the writing, the site changes, and the reporting. I worked across the firm, listening to the sales team about the clients they actually wanted and translating that into a plan the website could deliver.

This was a role where the strategy and the execution sat with the same person, which meant the plan never got lost in a hand-off.

ProcessHow I worked

I started by listening, to the sales team about their best clients and to the analytics about where attention leaked away. I audited every page for search visibility, mapped the terms real buyers use rather than the jargon we used internally, and studied how competitors framed the same services.

One idea held the plan together: meet the buyer at the question, not the pitch. Instead of shouting louder, Rockwater would become the clearest, most useful answer to what prospects were already searching for. That meant a content plan built around real buyer questions, a rebuilt site structure search engines could read, and paid media aimed at high-intent moments, each landing on a page designed to convert.

DeliverablesWhat I made
  • A rebuilt site structure and on-page SEO across priority service pages.
  • A content plan and articles answering the questions buyers ask before they enquire.
  • Reworked paid campaigns organized around intent, with rewritten landing pages.
  • A single reporting dashboard tying marketing activity to leads and conversations.
ResultsWhat moved

Organic visibility grew, and the leads got better as spend shifted toward intent. For the first time the team could point to marketing as a source of real conversations rather than clicks.

ReflectionWhat I learned

The biggest unlock was not a channel, it was language. The moment we started writing the way buyers actually talk, everything downstream got easier. If I ran it again, I would stand up the reporting dashboard on day one, because proving the story early earns budget faster.
